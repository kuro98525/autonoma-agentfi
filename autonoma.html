the deep dive

What an agent actually does, second by second

> the loop

Observe → decide → execute → settle.

Most of what you call "trading" is really just waiting. You wait to see the signal, wait to be free, wait for confirmation, wait to place the order. An agent removes the waiting. The loop — read the market, decide, size the position, settle it — runs in <1s, around the clock. As of late 2026, the fastest loops on Solana and Base settle faster than a human can blink.

Here's the shape of it. At 02:14 UTC a perp funding rate flips negative. A human is asleep. The agent reads the signal, sizes a hedge, and is flat before the candle closes. That isn't "AI magic" — it's a decision rule you set in advance, executed without hesitation or emotion.

The part most people miss: an agent doesn't have opinions. It has constraints. You give it an objective — "keep drawdown under a 6% vol cap" — and guardrails: position limits, stop-losses, a kill-switch. It optimizes inside the lines. It never gets greedy, never gets scared, never gets tired.

Stripped of hype, the pitch is one sentence: capital that keeps executing your rules when you can't. The question was never whether to automate. It's whose agent, and what rules.
